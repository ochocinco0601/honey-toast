# Purpose: teach people to use a tool or follow a method

**Fits when** the viewer should come away and *do* something with a thing they can operate: a
tool, a skill, a prompt, a procedure.

Its content comes from action mapping (Cathy Moore): start from what the viewer must *do*, and
include only what they need in order to do it.

## What to work out about the viewer before the brief

- **What goes wrong for them today without it, and once they use it, where do they go wrong?**
  Not a question for the person: work it out from the source, and from what the tool's own
  failure handling and checks reveal about where people trip. The source rarely says it outright.
  Write it as the brief's Problem line, where the person can object to it. This decides what two
  minutes are spent on.

## The lines this purpose adds to the brief

- **Problem.** What goes wrong for them today that this fixes, and where they get stuck.
- **Action.** What they do after watching that they did not do before. A verb they perform,
  such as "runs the check before saving". Never "understands X".
- **What the action needs.** Where the thing is, what to type or click, and what comes back to
  them to decide. How the tool works inside belongs here only where the viewer acts on it.

## Whether an explainer is the fix, for a tool

What usually stops people using a tool is not knowledge: they do not have it installed, cannot
sign in, have no credential, or the team has not filled in its local settings. Each of those goes
to the tool's owner. A tool that hands decisions back to its user, or takes more than about two
steps, gets a job aid.

**The job aid**, in this order:

1. **Before you start:** what must already be true, and where each comes from. Where that is
   decided at the viewer's workplace and the source does not say, write *unknown, ask the tool's
   owner*, never a guess.
2. **Start:** the first thing to open, and the first thing to type or click.
3. **What it hands back, and your answer:** each decision the tool returns to the viewer, in the
   order it arrives, and what a right answer looks like.
4. **Undo:** where, and what undoing also discards.

## The first moment, for a tool

Hand the walk these with the brief. Before scene 1, the viewer may have none of the tool open,
installed or signed in. Their first act is to open something (an application, a chat, a
terminal) and type or click a first thing in it. Every name, address, credential or permission
that first act needs is part of the walk.

## Rules for the script

- The opening (SKILL.md, "Every piece opens before it explains") names the tool and says in one
  line what the viewer will be able to do with it after watching.
- Every scene serves the action, or it is cut. This decides which scene goes when the budget is
  exceeded.
- The scenes follow the order the work reaches the viewer, not the order the tool is designed
  in. A piece organised around the tool's parts teaches how it is built, not the viewer's turns.
- The last scene shows the action concretely enough to perform: where, what to type, what comes
  back.

## The reviewer's outcome question

**Could someone who watched this do the action without asking anyone, and in the right order?**
Walk it as the viewer: after the last scene, what do they do first, second, third, and where do
they get stuck or go wrong?
