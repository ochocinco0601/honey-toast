# Readback

## The job this serves

**Two levels up:** the stakeholder (the person directing the work) steers it while sessions do the engineering, and sessions build from what the stakeholder meant, not from what a session guessed they meant. **One level up:** every record a later session reads carries the stakeholder's input as an understanding they have seen and confirmed, never their raw words and never a session's own prose about them. **This step:** turning what the stakeholder just said into a reading, recording it, and reading that recorded reading back to them in the same reply. The read-back is the part that must not be skipped.

**It receives** what the stakeholder said in the conversation, and the record the work already keeps (its issue, its work file or decision log; search the work's folder or tracker for it).

**It hands on** a four-part reading in that record, shown to the stakeholder in the same reply, with its confirmation state. Every later session that reads the record weighs it as input, not as a requirement.

**Done:** the stakeholder has seen the reading as recorded and confirmed or corrected it. A reading written down but not shown is not done.

**Field terms:** confirming elicitation results (business analysis); read-back (aviation); paraphrasing (active listening).

## When it applies

Both must hold:
1. **What they said is input:** a want, a concern, a correction, a boundary or an idea. Not an approval, an answer to a question you asked, or routine direction; those are acted on, not recorded.
2. **It is going into a record, or bears on a call that changes what they get** (its scope, its cost, or what a model decides on their behalf).

**Also:** when you find one of their readings still marked unconfirmed in a record that bears on what you are doing: put it to them again (step 4).

## The run

1. **Translate it into four parts.** Write it fresh; never paraphrase their sentence line by line. One message carrying several inputs gets one reading per input.
   - **Context:** what was being discussed when they said it.
   - **Basis:** the plain specifics it rests on: what happened, what they saw, what they wanted instead. Names, dates and examples they gave are kept.
   - **In the field's terms:** the established name for what this is an instance of. The term connects the specifics to a practice; it never replaces them.
   - **How meant:** a preference, a firm boundary, thinking aloud, or in frustration. A hedge stays a hedge; a point held more firmly than the rest is marked as firmer; frustration is recorded as the concern behind it.
2. **Write it into the record the work already has**, as its own block under a "Stakeholder input" heading, marked **unconfirmed**, with the date, time and session. A register or table links to the block instead of holding it. Never open a new issue only to hold a reading; if the work has no record yet, put it in the notes the work keeps for the next session.
3. **Read it back in the same reply.** After answering their message, end the reply with the reading exactly as you recorded it (your four parts, not their words), where it was written (a link), and "Does this fit?". What they see is what is on record.
4. **Act on their answer.**
   - **Yes:** mark it confirmed, with the time.
   - **A correction:** rewrite the four parts from it, replace the old reading, and show the new one, still unconfirmed.
   - **No answer:** it stays unconfirmed; silence is not a yes. Ask again when it next matters, and list it at the top of any handoff.
5. **Their words stay out of the record**, except wording they call a requirement in so many words ("this is a requirement", "keep this wording"), which is kept with a reading beneath it and is still checked against their other input and the scope. Emphasis, "need" or "must" does not make one.
6. **A framing of yours that they say resonated** is kept as written the same turn, as your own text, with the field's term beneath it; later corrections go in a dated section under it. Any term you coin carries the field's established term beside it.
7. **A reading is input, not a requirement.** A confirmed reading is still weighed against their other input and the scope before anything becomes a requirement. An unconfirmed one may be worked from, and you may undo your own over-reach on it at once, but it never decides scope, cost or what a model decides on their behalf.
8. **Skills and agent definitions** take the field's terms only: no reading block, no quotation, no pointer to where readings are kept.

## Gotchas

- **"Unconfirmed" is not a parking place.** A reading written but never read back is the failure this skill exists for.
- **The read-back is the record, not a summary of it.** Show the reading as recorded; a friendlier retelling hides what was actually written.
- **Your own prose is not a translation.** "They said in the same breath that..." narrates the conversation; the four parts translate it.
- **Turning input into rules in the same breath.** Do not restate their concern and then announce what the design now must do; it is input until weighed.
