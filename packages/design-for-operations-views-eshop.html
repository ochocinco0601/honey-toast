Design-for-operations views: home lender and eShop

An example of design-time views of a business capability, online retail, with the two capabilities inside it, drawn from the public source code of eShop, the open-source .NET reference application of an online store (github.com/dotnet/eShop, commit b4a4087). The levels are capability, intended flow, stage, process step, component and external dependency. eShop's AI shopping assistant is drawn as one of its components, and in view 4 as a system of its own. Each view is shown first on an invented home lender (views 1 and 4) or on the general form with placeholder names (views 2 and 3), then on eShop.

The words

LevelWhat it meansExample, from eShop
CapabilityWhat the business is able to do, named apart from how it is built. Never measured itself: it is covered by the flows that deliver it.Take and fulfil customer orders online
Intended flowOne case's path from start to end, as designed.Placing an order: one order
StageOne ordered stretch of a flow, with an entry and an exit.Take the order
Process stepOne act inside a stage, with an outcome the business would recognise, that happened or did not.The basket is emptied
ComponentA deployable part of the application that performs or supports a step.webapp; Shopping assistant
External dependencySomething the application relies on and is not part of.Webhook subscribers
